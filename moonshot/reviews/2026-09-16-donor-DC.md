# Against our sheet (read after the inventory)

## MISSING

- No opening count. Donors count the drawer at open and log expected/counted/difference before overwriting the float — `pos_session.py:1773-1789`, `:1810-1827`. Ours has a `float` movement nobody verifies: 11:00 Tuesday the till types ₹2,000 but the drawer holds ₹1,650 left from Monday; Tuesday closes ₹350 short and Tuesday's cashier wears it.
- No float carried from yesterday's **counted** close — `pos_session.py:372-380`. Out of scope says tomorrow's float is a fresh movement: 11:00 on the 17th nobody types it, the ₹2,000 still physically in the drawer is unaccounted, and 23:30 reads ₹2,000 over.
- No counted figure or difference for card and UPI. Donors take a counted amount per method and post its own difference — `pos_session.py:718-755`, `sub_pos_closing_payment/sub_pos_closing_payment.json:12-48`. Ours freezes card ₹31,200 as expected with nothing to count it against: the terminal batch at 23:40 reads ₹30,750, ₹450 of card sales never captured, discovered at the bank weeks later.
- Money that happened before the close but arrives after it has no home. Donors re-parent it to the open session and log it loudly — `pos_order.py:31-53`. An offline till takes ₹400 cash at 23:25, syncs at 23:48 into a day closed at 23:30, and R6 refuses it: the payment is simply lost.
- No path for a refund against a closed day's bill. Donors require an open session and land the refund in the **current** one — `pos_order.py:1434-1437`. 12:30 the next day a guest returns for ₹609 on last night's bill and the cashier has no button.
- Nothing nudges a day left open, and nothing stops several days being open at once — `pos_session.py:312-320`, `:1844-1857`. The 14th is never closed; at 09:00 on the 17th a ₹450 cash take lands on a business date whose drawer was emptied three days ago.
- Closing does not tell the other devices — `pos_session.py:95-103`. At 23:31 the captain's tablet still shows the day open, takes ₹400 in notes from a guest, and the server refuses it after the money has changed hands.
- No per-cashier close at a handover. URY gives each cashier their own period, own invoices and own count, and blocks the day close over an open one — `doctype/sub_pos_closing/sub_pos_closing.py:14-46`, `hooks/ury_pos_closing_entry.py:11-33`. Ravi takes Priya's drawer at 18:00 uncounted; at 23:30 it is ₹800 short and `byStaff` cannot say whose shift.

## DIFFERENT

- Over/short threshold: donors block the close above the authorised difference unless a manager is there — `pos_session.py:816-817`, `pos_config.py:166-169` (and theirs is a client-side check, so ours is safer against tampering). Ours only varies audit severity (DC-S23). For the money, a server-side block plus a second PIN is safer: at 23:00 a lone cashier who is ₹4,500 short closes and goes home, and the difference is witnessed by nobody.
- Wrong cash movement: donors delete the row under a stricter right and log the delete — `pos_session.py:1894-1904`, `res_users.py:28-30`. Ours voids and never deletes (DC-S10). Ours is safer: the mistyped ₹12,000 is still on the screen at 09:00 next morning next to the ₹1,200 that replaced it.
- Difference handling: donors post it to a profit/loss account and **fail the close** if that account is missing — `pos_session.py:486-528`. Ours writes the number and an audit row. Ours is safer for a lone cashier at 11pm, who can never be locked out of closing by a config gap she cannot fix.
- Close atomicity: donors run a multi-step transaction with a rollback and a manager force-close wizard — `pos_session.py:446-458`, `:578-596`. Ours is one `create` on a date-id document (R9). Ours is safer: a single create cannot half-close, and no force path means no manager-shaped escape hatch.
- Second closer: donors tell the loser the day was closed by someone else **and give the later sales a rescue session** — `pos_session.py:730-740`. Ours refuses both the second close and the later sale (DC-S6/S7). Ours is safer for the count, theirs is safer for the money, because the ₹400 at 23:45 still lands somewhere.
- Blind count: URY gates expected and difference by permission level, so the counter is blind while a manager can still see them — `sub_pos_closing_payment.json:31-47`. Ours omits `expectedCash` from every role until close (R14). Ours is safer against collusion; theirs is safer at 11pm when the lone closer *is* the manager and has nothing to reconcile against.
- Freezing: donors freeze by state plus an accounting lock date but still allow a manager force-close or an Admin amend — `pos_session.py:322-330`, `sub_pos_closing.json` permissions. Ours: nobody, ever (R11). Safest for the money; a fat-fingered ₹13,616 count is then wrong forever and only corrected sideways on the next open day.

## OURS ALONE

- Open is the **absence** of a document; there is no `closed: false`. Donors carry an explicit state enum — `pos_session.py:23-27`.
- The business date is the document id and `create` is the entire concurrency control, with no read-then-write anywhere — `pos_session.py:391-412`, `sub_pos_closing.py:70-74`.
- An unreadable close document means *unknown*, and every caller refuses on unknown (R1) — donors treat a failed or empty read as closeable — `pos_session.py:718-755`.
- The close refuses outright when the payment ledger cannot be read (DC-S13); donors compute the day from whatever rows come back — `pos_session.py:247-264`.
- A closed day retroactively refuses new **takes and refunds**, not only edits (R6); donors redirect them instead — `pos_order.py:31-53`.
- `overpaid` is its own frozen per-tender number (DC-S15); the donors' only overpay concept is a change flag on a tender row — `pos_payment.py:42`.
- The threshold in force is frozen onto the close document so severity stays explainable after the key changes (DC-S23); donors read live config — `pos_config.py:166-169`.
