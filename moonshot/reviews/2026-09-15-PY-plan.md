# PY · Payments — build plan (draft for Shaurya, 2026-09-15)

**Job.** Take a finalised bill to paid. Record every act of money changing hands: tenders, a bill split by amount across several of them, change on cash, a refund against a credit note, and a paid mark nothing else in the system can set. PY is the ledger. UPI QR and card terminals are later modules that call into it; PY is not the pipe.

## What exists today, honestly

`orders/orderConstants.js:14` is the whole of it:

```js
exports.PAYMENT_STATUS = { UNPAID: 'unpaid', PARTIALLY_PAID: 'partially_paid', PAID: 'paid' };
```

One string on the order document. Every writer of it, in production code:

| Where | What it does |
|---|---|
| `orders/createOrUpdateOrder.js:304` | sets `unpaid` at checkout |
| `orders/updateOrderStatus.js:156` | sets `paid` as a **side effect** of `orderStatus → COMPLETED` |

That is both of them. Readers: `adminApp/orders_admin.js:102,181` and the admin Flutter app (`orders_api_service.dart:143,190` → `orders_screen.dart:320,389`, a coloured chip). Kitchen and server apps never read it.

What is wrong with it, in the order it will hurt:

1. **Payment rides on the order lifecycle field.** Critical pieces §3: kitchen progress, service and payment are three independent axes. Here, tapping Complete marks the money received. You cannot say "served, not paid" — which is exactly the go-live blocker already logged in `STATE.md` — or "paid, food still coming".
2. **No money is recorded anywhere.** A `paid` order does not say how much was taken, in what, by whom, or at what minute. A cash drawer cannot be counted against it, so day close has nothing to reconcile.
3. **`partially_paid` is unreachable.** No code ever writes it. It appears only in mock seeds. Split by amount cannot be expressed at all.
4. **There is no refund.** BL-S11 issues a credit note for the coke that was never served; nothing gives the guest their ₹84 back or records that it happened.
5. **Paid moves backwards.** `updateOrderStatus` accepts any status any number of times; nothing guards COMPLETED → PENDING. Critical pieces §3 says a paid bill cannot move backwards.
6. **Money is float rupees.** `priceInfo.finalPrice` is a rounded float. ST and BL are integer minor units. Payments are the one place a half-paisa becomes a drawer that does not balance.
7. **A phantom fourth value.** Both read paths default a missing field to `'pending'`, which is not in the enum. The admin chip shows a state the backend can never produce.

So PY is not an extension of `paymentStatus`. It is a new ledger, and `order.paymentStatus` becomes a one-line mirror kept alive for the admin app.

## The shape I intend

**A payment is an event, not a state.** One document per act of money moving:

`restaurants/{id}/payments/{paymentId}` — append only, nothing ever edited or deleted.

`{paymentId, billId, cid, kind: 'take' | 'refund', tenderId, amount, tendered, change, at, by, ref, creditNoteId?}`

`amount` is positive minor units in both kinds; `kind` carries the sign. `tendered` and `change` are cash-only and null otherwise. `ref` is the free string a card slip or UPI reference goes in.

**Paid is derived, then stamped.** `outstanding = bill.payable − (Σ take − Σ refund)`. The transaction that writes the tender taking outstanding to zero also sets `bill.status = 'paid'` and `bill.paidTotal`. Two tills racing on the same bill: the transaction re-reads and re-decides, the second one is refused as overpayment. Same pattern as ST's arch-2 fix.

**Nothing else can set paid.** `api/payments.ts` is the only writer of `bill.status = 'paid'`, and PY never calls `updateOrderStatus`.

## Calls I will make unless you say otherwise

- **Tenders are config, not an enum.** `payments.tenders[]` on the config doc: `{id, label, kind: 'cash' | 'external', opensDrawer, needsRef}`. Cash, card and UPI are three rows in a default, not three branches. A new tender is a config edit. `domain/` never sees the word "UPI".
- **Money is integer minor units** throughout, as in ST and BL. The till sends rupees as typed, the API rounds to minor units once, at the edge. Same rule as ST-S2's amount.
- **No rounding inside PY.** BL already rounded the payable once (its R7). A tender is compared against that number exactly. See open question 2 — this is the accountant's to confirm.
- **Overpayment is refused on external tenders, change on cash.** ₹700 handed over for a ₹609 bill: take 700, change 91, paid. A ₹700 card tap on a ₹609 bill is `invalid-argument`. Tips are out of scope v1 and named in the sheet.
- **A refund only exists against a credit note.** No free-standing refunds. `creditNoteId` is required on `kind: 'refund'`, and the refund may not exceed that note's total. The credit note is BL's, the money is PY's.
- **PY does not touch the table.** Today `updateOrderStatus` calls `vacateTable` when an order completes. PY's paid mark frees nothing; releasing a table when no open bill remains in its group is OR's, and it is already a known blocker. Logged as debt, not smuggled in here.
- **`order.paymentStatus` keeps working.** One line in `app/payments.ts` mirrors PY's derived state onto the order doc (`unpaid` / `partially_paid` / `paid` — `partially_paid` finally becomes reachable). The field is mirrored, never read back and never extended. `DEBT(TD-010)`.
- **Out of scope v1, in the sheet:** gateway integrations of any kind, tips, loyalty and wallet redemption, cards on file, settlement and reconciliation files, foreign currency, part-payment across two bills, deposits and advances.

## Existing code I will touch, and how little

- `index.js` — three exports.
- `app/payments.ts` — the one mirror line above.
- Nothing else. `updateOrderStatus.js` keeps setting `paid` on COMPLETED for now; the two writers disagreeing is ugly and is exactly why the mirror is written as debt rather than as a design. Taking the side effect **out** of `updateOrderStatus` is a change to an existing dir and needs a characterization test first — I would rather do it as its own commit once the till can actually take money, not while PY has no UI. Say the word if you want it in v1.

## Open questions for Shaurya (each has a default; say nothing and I take it)

1. **Who may refund?** Default: MANAGER and ADMIN, PIN every time, reason from the ST list. SERVER gets 403. Same gate as cancelling a bill. *(On my stop-and-ask list, so I am asking — but this default is the lazy one and I will take it.)*
2. **Rounding per tender.** Default: none. BL rounds the payable once, and PY compares against that exact number. If the accountant wants cash rounded to the rupee separately from card, that is a config key (`payments.cashRoundTo`) and one extra scenario. **This is a money rule I should not invent.**
3. **Refund tender.** Default: cash only in v1. Reversing a card or UPI payment is the gateway modules' job and does not exist yet, so a card refund is handed over as cash and the row says so. Alternative: allow a `card` refund row as a bookkeeping entry with no money movement, which reconciles worse. **Accountant / ops call.**
4. **Split by amount — how far.** Default: unlimited tender rows per bill up to `payments.maxTendersPerBill` (10), each any amount, any tender. Split by lines is BL-S12 and already belongs there. Splitting evenly "three ways" is arithmetic the till does before it sends three rows; the backend only sees amounts.
5. **Does paying free the table?** Default: no, PY changes no table state (see above). If you want the till to feel like today's app, that is one call from the till to OR's existing endpoint, not a line in PY.

## Config keys I will add (defaults in brackets)

`payments.tenders[]` (cash, card, upi) · `payments.allowPartial` (true) · `payments.refundNeedsPin` (true) · `payments.maxTendersPerBill` (10) · `payments.cashRoundTo` (0, off) · `payments.reasons` for refunds (reuses `approvals.reasons`).

## Portability

The BL plan page's knobs apply here unchanged. Nothing in `domain/payments.ts` contains a currency symbol, a tender name, or a country. A tender is `{id, kind}` where `kind` is `cash` or `external`, because change-making is the only behaviour the maths cares about. `locale.minorUnits` stays the only place the unit count lives.
