**1. Composition scheme vs regular GST for a restaurant.**
**Summary:** A restaurant serving liquor is legally barred from the GST composition scheme.
**Answer:** Because the restaurant serves alcohol (a non-taxable supply), it is ineligible for the composition scheme. If a restaurant *were* eligible and on the scheme, it may not charge or show any tax on the bill; it must issue a "Bill of Supply" (not a tax invoice) and must mandatorily print the words: "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies".
**Effective Date:** 1 July 2017 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Section 10(2)(b) and Section 31(3)(c) of the CGST Act, 2017; CGST Rule 46.
**Confidence:** High.

**2. GST rate on restaurant food and drink service.**
**Summary:** Standalone restaurants charge 5% GST without ITC, whereas hotels with room tariffs over ₹7,500 charge 18% with ITC.
**Answer:** A standalone restaurant applies a 5% GST rate without the benefit of Input Tax Credit (ITC). A restaurant inside a hotel applies 5% (no ITC) if the hotel's declared room tariff is ₹7,500 or less per day, but 18% (with ITC) if the tariff exceeds ₹7,500 per day. The deciding factor is solely the hotel room tariff. 
**Effective Date:** 15 November 2017 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) as amended.
**Confidence:** High.

**3. Karnataka tax on liquor sold for consumption in a bar or restaurant.**
**Summary:** Liquor retail sales are taxed at 0% on the bill, as Karnataka collects revenue upfront via an AIB-based excise duty.
**Answer:** Liquor is governed by state excise law, and there is no VAT or sales tax added by the restaurant. The current retail tax rate applied on sales to a guest is 0% (NIL), as the state collects tax upfront at the wholesale/distributor level (KSBCL). The tax structure last changed on 11 May 2026 to an Alcohol-in-Beverage (AIB) excise structure (VAT itself was abolished long prior, in 2017).
**Effective Date:** 11 May 2026 (Excise restructure); 1 April 2017 (VAT abolition).
**Basis:** Karnataka Excise Act, 1965.
**Confidence:** High.

**4. Tax-inclusive pricing vs tax added on liquor in Karnataka.**
**Summary:** Liquor is priced tax-inclusive on the menu, with no state tax shown on the final bill.
**Answer:** Liquor is conventionally and legally priced tax-inclusive. Because no retail VAT or GST applies to liquor in Karnataka, no tax is added on the final bill. The law requires the bill to merely show the sale value of the beverage.
**Effective Date:** 1 April 2017 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Karnataka Excise Act, 1965 (absence of VAT/GST levy at retail).
**Confidence:** High.

**5. Service charge added by a restaurant.**
**Summary:** Service charge is part of the GST taxable value, but adding it by default is illegal.
**Answer:** If collected, a service charge forms part of the taxable value and is taxed at the restaurant's GST rate (5%). However, it is legally prohibited for a restaurant to add a service charge by default, and it must be removed upon customer request. While some restaurants in common practice still attempt to add it by default, the law strictly forbids it.
**Effective Date:** July 2022 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Section 15 of the CGST Act, 2017 (for taxability); Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) Guidelines (upheld by the Delhi High Court).
**Confidence:** High.

**6. Single bill for GST and liquor vs separate bills.**
**Summary:** A single bifurcated bill is legally acceptable, though separate bills are common practice.
**Answer:** A single printed bill carrying both a GST block for food and a non-taxable block for liquor is legally acceptable as an "invoice-cum-bill of supply" provided the taxable and non-taxable supplies are clearly bifurcated. In common practice, many restaurants prefer to issue two separate bills or series to simplify accounting and POS reporting, but separate bills are not a legal mandate.
**Effective Date:** 1 July 2017 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Rule 46A of the CGST Rules, 2017.
**Confidence:** High.

**7. HSN or SAC codes on a B2C restaurant bill.**
**Summary:** HSN/SAC codes are optional on B2C bills for turnover under ₹5 crore, with SAC 996331 being the relevant code.
**Answer:** For a B2C transaction (walk-in guests) where the restaurant's aggregate turnover is under ₹5 crore, it is entirely optional to print the HSN/SAC code on the bill. If a restaurant chooses to print it, the applicable code for restaurant services is SAC 996331, and applying it at the bill level is sufficient since it is not legally mandated.
**Effective Date:** 1 April 2021 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Notification No. 78/2020-Central Tax (amending Notification 12/2017-CT).
**Confidence:** High.

**8. Discounts and taxable value.**
**Summary:** Pre-supply printed discounts reduce GST taxable value, but post-supply discounts for B2C guests do not.
**Answer:** A discount printed on the bill against a line before or at the time of supply reduces the GST taxable value. However, a post-supply discount given after the invoice is issued cannot reduce the GST taxable value for B2C transactions, because the law requires a prior agreement and the reversal of Input Tax Credit (ITC) by the recipient—which is impossible for an unregistered walk-in guest.
**Effective Date:** 1 July 2017 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Section 15(3)(a) and Section 15(3)(b) of the CGST Act, 2017.
**Confidence:** High.

**9. GST tax invoice for B2C: fields, numbering, and rounding.**
**Summary:** Specific supplier and tax fields are mandatory, numbering must be consecutive/unique per FY, and rounding is permitted.
**Answer:** Mandatory fields for a B2C tax invoice include: supplier name, address, GSTIN, consecutive serial number, date, description of service, value of supply, rate of tax, and tax amount. Invoice numbering must be consecutive, unique for a financial year, and not exceed 16 characters (containing alphabets, numerals, hyphens, or slashes). Rounding the total payable tax and final amount to the nearest rupee is explicitly permitted.
**Effective Date:** 1 July 2017 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017 (fields and numbering); Section 170 of the CGST Act, 2017 (rounding).
**Confidence:** High.

**10. Credit notes under GST for a restaurant.**
**Summary:** Credit notes adjust finalized invoices, must reference the original invoice, and have a 30th November deadline.
**Answer:** A credit note must be issued instead of cancelling a bill once the original tax invoice has been finalized, issued, or the supply recorded, and the taxable value or tax charged needs to be reduced. It must explicitly reference the original tax invoice number(s) and date(s). To declare it and reduce output tax liability, the deadline is the 30th of November of the following financial year, or the date of furnishing the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier.
**Effective Date:** 1 October 2022 to Present (15 September 2026).
**Basis:** Section 34 of the CGST Act, 2017 (amended by Finance Act 2022); Rule 53 of the CGST Rules, 2017.
**Confidence:** High.
