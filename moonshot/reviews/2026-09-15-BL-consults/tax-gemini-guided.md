1. **Composition scheme vs regular GST**: A composition restaurant cannot collect tax and must issue a "Bill of Supply" bearing the words "Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" at the top.
- **Effective**: 1 July 2017 onwards.
- **Basis**: Section 10 of CGST Act, 2017; Rule 5(1)(f) and Rule 49 of CGST Rules, 2017.
- **Confidence**: High.

2. **GST rate on restaurant service**: Standalone restaurants apply 5% GST without Input Tax Credit (ITC), while restaurants inside a hotel with any room tariff of INR 7,500 or more apply 18% GST with ITC.
- **Effective**: 15 November 2017 (via amending notification).
- **Basis**: Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), heavily amended by Notification No. 46/2017-Central Tax (Rate).
- **Confidence**: High.

3. **Karnataka tax on liquor**: Liquor is outside GST and is taxed under the Karnataka Sales Tax (KST) Act and state excise duty (which shifted to an Alcohol-in-Beverage structure in May 2026), though the exact KST rate at the B2C restaurant level is UNVERIFIED.
- **Effective**: KST Act applies continually; AIB excise duty from 11 May 2026.
- **Basis**: Karnataka Sales Tax Act, 1957; Karnataka Excise Act, 1965.
- **Confidence**: Medium (High on the laws, Low/Unverified on the exact current KST percentage rate for a CL-9 bar).

4. **Inclusive vs exclusive liquor pricing**: By common practice, menu prices for liquor are tax-inclusive and establishments back out the KST on the bill, but the law requires any tax collected from the customer to be clearly determinable on the bill.
- **Effective**: Current period.
- **Basis**: Karnataka Sales Tax Rules / local commercial tax practice.
- **Confidence**: Medium.

5. **Service charge**: A restaurant cannot add a service charge by default and must remove it on request; if voluntarily paid by the customer, it becomes part of the taxable value and is subject to the restaurant's GST rate (e.g., 5%).
- **Effective**: CCPA Guidelines on 4 July 2022 (upheld 2025); GST valuation from 1 July 2017.
- **Basis**: CCPA Guidelines; Section 15(2)(c) of CGST Act, 2017.
- **Confidence**: High.

6. **One combined bill**: A single printed document containing both GST (food) and state non-taxable (liquor) items is legally permissible and is termed an "invoice-cum-bill of supply" for unregistered customers.
- **Effective**: 13 October 2017.
- **Basis**: Rule 46A of the CGST Rules, 2017.
- **Confidence**: High.

7. **HSN/SAC codes on B2C bill**: For a B2C restaurant with a turnover under INR 5 crore, displaying the SAC code (9963) on the bill is optional (zero digits required).
- **Effective**: 1 April 2021.
- **Basis**: Notification No. 78/2020-Central Tax.
- **Confidence**: High.

8. **Discounts**: A discount printed on the bill at the time of supply reduces the GST taxable value, but a post-sale discount given after B2C billing cannot reduce the taxable value since the unregistered customer cannot reverse Input Tax Credit.
- **Effective**: 1 July 2017.
- **Basis**: Section 15(3)(a) and 15(3)(b) of the CGST Act, 2017.
- **Confidence**: High.

9. **B2C tax invoice rules**: A bill must contain supplier details, a unique consecutive serial number per financial year (max 16 characters, allowing hyphens/slashes), date, description, value, and tax rate/amount; rounding the total payable to the nearest rupee is permitted.
- **Effective**: 1 July 2017.
- **Basis**: Rule 46(b) of CGST Rules, 2017; Section 170 of CGST Act, 2017.
- **Confidence**: High.

10. **Credit notes**: A credit note must be issued if the tax was overcharged or service was deficient after the invoice was recorded in returns, it must reference the original invoice(s), and the deadline is 30th November of the following financial year.
- **Effective**: 1 October 2022 (deadline amendment).
- **Basis**: Section 34 of the CGST Act, 2017.
- **Confidence**: High.
